EIDORA — The Embedding
Space Explorer

Open-source desktop research software for the visual exploration of embedding spaces in cultural data.

EIDORA supports local, project-based work with media, metadata, vector representations, projections, annotations, and figures. It is designed for researchers who need to inspect computational representations while keeping interpretation grounded in domain knowledge.

Five connected workspaces

From data to exploration to figures.

Five connected workspaces bring the stages of embedding space analysis together. Start with your source material, or bring existing embeddings and projections into your project.

01 / Project

Organize your source material

Add media sources, link them to metadata records, and prepare thumbnail sets for visual exploration. Keep your source material connected to the items in your project.

02 / Metadata

Work with the context

Import, inspect, and edit metadata. Add items, update descriptions, and organize custom groups that you can use throughout your analysis.

03 / Embeddings

Create and compare representations

Browse the Model Zoo and run compatible models locally, or import existing embeddings. Manage vector datasets and run clustering methods to investigate their structure.

04 / Discovery

Navigate the embedding space

Create projections, explore points and thumbnails, and use metadata to filter and color your view. Add annotations, move through ordered dimensions with the Slicer, and save views as snapshots.

05 / Figures

Compose and share your findings

Arrange snapshots into figures and animations. Adjust compositions, captions, and appearance, then export the results. Saved compositions remain available when you reopen the project.

From overview to individual items

Explore patterns
in context.

Move between an overview of a collection and the individual items within it. Use metadata to investigate how patterns relate to time, categories, and other dimensions.

For cultural data, this connects visual exploration with historical descriptions, collection records, and the questions you bring to the material.

Nearby points suggest relationships within a model’s representation. Their meaning still calls for close reading, context, and your judgment.

01

Return to an exploration

Save a view as a snapshot, including its projection, camera position, filters, annotations, and appearance. Restore it to continue your analysis, compare views, or compose a figure.

02

Move through time and other dimensions

Use the Slicer to explore ordered metadata through a rolling window. Set its size and step interval, then observe how different subsets occupy the same projection while the coordinates remain fixed.

03

Start at any stage

Begin with source media, precomputed embeddings, or existing projection coordinates. Export intermediate results for work in other tools and bring them back into your project.

Good to know.

Which platforms will EIDORA support?

The release targets are macOS on Apple Silicon and Intel, Windows x64, and Linux x64. Confirmed operating-system requirements and installation instructions will accompany the public release.

Do I need to write code?

EIDORA provides a graphical desktop environment for importing data, computing embeddings, exploring projections, and composing figures. Choosing models and interpreting their results remains part of the research process.

How does EIDORA work with my data?

EIDORA organizes research in local projects, bringing together media sources, metadata, computational results, and saved exploration states. Compatible models run on your device after installation. Downloading models requires an internet connection; local analysis does not depend on a cloud service.

What can an embedding map tell me?

A map can help you notice neighborhoods, clusters, and unusual items worth investigating. Its shape depends on your data, model, and projection settings. Treat it as a starting point for interpretation, alongside the original material and its context.

Is EIDORA open source?

Yes. EIDORA’s source code is available under the MIT license. The project repository includes information for developers and contributors.
